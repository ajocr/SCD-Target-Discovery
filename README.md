# SCD-Target-Discovery

## Description

Data-driven identification and prioritization of underexplored therapeutic targets for fetal hemoglobin reactivation in sickle cell patients

## Background

Sickle Cell Disease (SCD) is a group of inherited blood diseases in which a child inherits at least one is the abnormal protein hemoglobin(HB) S. It affects approx, 6.5 to 9 million individuals worldwide; 90% being in Africa and India.
In sickle cell patients, HBF (fetal hemoglobin) is deactivated a few months after birth, leading to just the production of HBS.
Drug development for SCD has faced numerous setbacks, however there is hope in future breakthroughs with the promising early results of Casgevy.
The reality is this therapy is extremely expensive and largely inaccessible to the majority of the population affected by the disease. This means there is need to keep researching possibilities.
The issue stems from the overproduction of HBS and the deactivition of HBF. This research hopes to determine the the efficiacy of other targets besides BCL11A

## Research Questions

1. What is the current therapeutic landscape for HbF reactivation: gene therapy vs small molecule approaches?
2. How does genetic evidence and bioactivity data for KLF1 and c-Myb compare to BCL11A as drug targets?
3. Beyond BCL11A, which HbF-regulating targets show the strongest druggability potential with an acceptable safety profile?
